# Office Hours: SupplyTap

**Mode:** startup diagnostic, pre-product. **Verdict:** build only as a sales-learning instrument for independent distributors, not as evidence that another QR inventory SaaS deserves to exist.

## Six forcing questions

1. **Demand reality:** Reddit pain and incumbent adoption prove the workflow exists, but there is no direct proof that an independent distributor will pay SupplyTap. The strongest available signal is incumbents' paid inventory products and Grainger's reported stockout savings. Three paid pilots are the threshold that changes this conclusion.
2. **Status quo:** a worker notices an empty bin, texts or calls an admin/rep, the buyer reconstructs SKU and site details, then panic-orders. Free labels and distributor calls are the actual competitor.
3. **Desperate specificity:** the initial buyer is the owner or sales VP of a $5M–$50M regional janitorial distributor losing account stickiness to Grainger. The daily user is the customer's facilities lead whose credibility suffers when soap, liners, towels, or abrasives run out.
4. **Narrowest paid wedge:** one distributor, one customer site, 50 pre-mapped SKUs, scan-to-approval, and a human-confirmed order email. No marketplace, forecasting engine, or ERP integration.
5. **Observation plan:** install ten fictional-but-realistic labels in a pilot closet and watch five workers scan without instruction. Record wrong-item scans, duplicate scans, quantity changes, abandoned approvals, and time-to-acknowledgement. A guided demo does not count.
6. **Future-fit:** self-service purchasing makes the interface more normal, but QR scanning alone becomes commodity. The durable asset would be distributor-specific SKU mapping, reorder history, account rules, and measured stockout reduction.

## Premise challenge

- The product wins only if it increases distributor retention or account share; convenience alone will be copied.
- The first customer is the distributor, not the facility, because existing procurement relationships are the adoption shortcut.
- The prototype should prove a complete operational handoff and expose exceptions visibly.

## Approaches considered

### A — Scan-to-email pilot (recommended)
Small static UI plus validated order recommendation and approval. **Effort:** S. **Risk:** medium. Fastest path to observed behavior and a paid pilot.

### B — White-label distributor portal
Multi-account catalog mapping, branding, roles, and order export. **Effort:** L. **Risk:** high. Better commercial shape, but premature before channel proof.

### C — Supplier-neutral procurement network
Marketplace, catalog normalization, fulfillment routing, and payments. **Effort:** XL. **Risk:** extreme. Theoretical network effects, but fights procurement and incumbents.

## Assignment

Put the working scan-to-approval demo in front of ten independent distributor owners. Ask for a paid 30-day, one-site pilot; count deposits, not compliments.
